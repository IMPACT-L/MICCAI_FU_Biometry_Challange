\begin{abstract}
\noindent Ultrasound biometry requires accurate localization of anatomical landmarks across fetal, intrapartum, and cardiac imaging protocols. The target structures range from short endpoint measurements to dense multi-landmark cardiac configurations, while ultrasound appearance varies across devices, acquisition settings, and patients. We propose a unified coarse-to-fine landmark localization framework that combines a shared DINOv3 vision transformer encoder, task-specific feature pyramid necks, context-local task adaptation, high-resolution heatmaps, and learned offset refinement. We further introduce content-aware anatomical retrieval refinement, which normalizes the ultrasound field of view and uses similar training cases as a conservative inference-time geometric prior. On the released annotation consistency evaluation covering nine task subsets, the selected DINOv3 Vision Transformer Base (ViT-B) model achieves 5.64-pixel average MRE. On public validation, the full pipeline improves over an early ViT-L task-FPN baseline from 29.88 to 24.03. These results show that pretrained transformer features, high-resolution offset refinement, and content-normalized anatomical priors provide an effective foundation for general ultrasound biometry.

\keywords{Ultrasound Biometry \and Multi-Task Learning \and DINOv3 \and Landmark Localization \and Vision Transformers}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Ultrasound biometry converts anatomical appearance into reproducible geometric measurements, such as endpoints, diameters, angles, and multi-landmark contours. These measurements are central to fetal, intrapartum, and cardiac assessment, but their automation remains difficult because ultrasound images contain speckle, shadowing, missing boundaries, device-dependent contrast, and strong protocol variation. This makes multi-domain ultrasound biometry a useful test case for whether a single model can combine general visual representation learning with precise, task-specific geometric prediction.

The Foundation Model for Ultrasound Biometry benchmark exposes this difficulty. A single submission must process nine subsets spanning fetal, intrapartum, and adult cardiac ultrasound. Some subsets require only two anatomical endpoints, whereas others require dense multi-landmark configurations over a wider field of view. The official objective combines landmark Mean Radial Error (MRE) with derived measurement Mean Absolute Error (MAE), so a useful model must localize points accurately and preserve the geometry needed for clinical measurements. Separate anatomy-specific networks can specialize, but they do not test whether one foundation-style model can support all tasks. A fully shared decoder has the opposite problem: it can share data efficiently, but may ignore the different geometry of short segments, compact diameters, and dense cardiac configurations.

We address this trade-off with a unified coarse-to-fine landmark model. The system combines three technical elements: a shared DINOv3 encoder with task-specific spatial aggregation, high-resolution heatmap-plus-offset decoding for sub-pixel landmarks, and a deterministic content-aware retrieval refinement for residual layout shift. The retrieval module normalizes the ultrasound field of view, retrieves same-task training cases with similar content, and uses their landmark geometry as a gated anatomical prior while preserving the learned model weights. The main contribution is therefore a complete single-model design for heterogeneous ultrasound landmark biometry, together with an inference-time anatomical refinement module for residual domain-shift errors.

\section{Related Work}
Ultrasound biometry has traditionally been organized around anatomy- and protocol-specific measurements. Recent clinical guidelines define standardized fetal and cardiac ultrasound views and specify how geometric quantities should be obtained from these images~\cite{isuog2023cardiac}. Deep learning has also been used for ultrasound view recognition and localization, as in SonoNet for fetal standard plane analysis~\cite{baumgartner2017sononet}. These works demonstrate the value of task-specific ultrasound modeling, but the setting considered here is broader: the model must handle multiple anatomical domains and landmark geometries within one submission pipeline.

Landmark localization provides a natural representation for this problem because points can be evaluated directly and converted into downstream biometric measurements. Heatmap regression has been a dominant approach for accurate landmark and keypoint prediction because it preserves spatial uncertainty and provides dense supervision~\cite{payer2016regressing,newell2016hourglass,sun2019hrnet}. Center-based and object-as-points formulations further show that compact dense prediction can recover precise coordinates from image features~\cite{zhou2019objects}. Structured landmark methods are especially relevant for ultrasound because they incorporate spatial configuration when local appearance is ambiguous~\cite{payer2019integrating}.
Recent DINO-based medical tracking work also shows that self-supervised transformer features can provide robust explicit correspondences for anatomical motion estimation in cine medical imaging~\cite{salari2025dinomotion}.
Our method follows this heatmap-based line, but extends it with high-resolution outputs and learned offsets so that the final coordinates are less limited by heatmap discretization.

The encoder design is motivated by progress in self-supervised vision transformers. ViT, DINO, DINOv2, and DINOv3 show that transformer representations can transfer across visual domains and support dense downstream prediction~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit,caron2021dino,oquab2024dinov2,simeoni2025dinov3}. Recent ultrasound studies from our group also showed the value of multi-task supervision, semi-supervised representation learning, and prompt-driven foundation models for ultrasound analysis~\cite{behboodi2021breast,rasaee2025sarcopenia,rasaee2025grounding}. For our landmark task, the model must transform transferable image features into a small set of measurement-defining points. We therefore use a DINOv3 vision transformer as the shared encoder and attach task-specific spatial prediction modules for landmark decoding through PyTorch Image Models~\cite{timm}.

The multi-task aspect is equally important. Classical hard parameter sharing can improve data efficiency but can also underfit tasks with different label structure, whereas cross-stitch connections and uncertainty-based weighting provide mechanisms for balancing related objectives~\cite{caruana1997,misra2016crossstitch,kendall2018multitask}. The Foundation Model for Ultrasound Biometry setting has exactly this tension: all tasks share ultrasound statistics, but their landmark counts, spatial layouts, and measurement semantics differ substantially. Our architecture therefore keeps one shared pretrained encoder while allowing task-specific feature aggregation and task-conditioned decoding where geometry requires it.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem-formulation}
Each training image belongs to one of nine task subsets: apical four-chamber view (A4C), angle of progression (AOP), fetal abdomen (FA), the released two-point cervical-length subset (FUGC), head circumference (HC), inferior vena cava (IVC), parasternal long-axis view (PLAX), parasternal short-axis view (PSAX), or fetal femur length (\texttt{fetal\_femur}). For an image $I_i$ with task label $t_i$, the annotation is a set of $K_{t_i}$ two-dimensional landmarks $\mathbf{Y}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{K_{t_i}\times 2}$, where $K_t$ ranges from two to twenty-two. We cast all subsets into a common heatmap-based landmark regression framework. The task identity determines the number of active landmarks, the feature neck, and the decoder, while the encoder and optimization pipeline remain shared. All landmark coordinates are transformed into the letterboxed input coordinate system during training and are transformed back to the original image system for evaluation export.

\subsection{Model overview}
The final architecture is a single multi-task network with a shared DINOv3 ViT-B encoder. Given an input image and its task identifier, the encoder first produces a global transformer feature representation. A task-specific feature pyramid network (FPN) neck then converts this representation into a dense feature map with stronger spatial detail. The task-conditioned adapter injects local image evidence and task-group context before the selected decoder predicts landmark heatmaps. The model uses 512\,$\times$\,512 letterboxed inputs and 128\,$\times$\,128 output heatmaps in the selected configuration. The main design principle is coarse-to-fine prediction: the heatmap branch estimates a spatial probability field for each landmark, and a learned offset branch refines the soft-argmax coordinate to reduce heatmap discretization error. \subsection{Shared backbone and feature neck}
Our main model family uses a pretrained DINOv3 visual transformer as the shared encoder. The selected architecture uses DINOv3 ViT-B, which provides broad anatomical and appearance context while remaining practical for the single-model multi-task setting. This global context is important because several landmarks are ambiguous when considered from local texture alone.

The feature pyramid neck recovers the spatial resolution needed for point prediction. The selected model uses task-specific FPNs, one per task subset, while keeping the DINOv3 encoder shared. This preserves common ultrasound representation learning in the backbone, but allows each task to choose a different balance between semantic context and boundary detail. This is important because dense cardiac landmarking, compact head circumference prediction, vascular endpoints, and fetal bone endpoints do not require the same feature scale.

After the FPN, the selected model applies a context-local adapter. This adapter receives both the FPN feature map and the input image, and produces a residual task-conditioned feature correction. The purpose is to combine transformer-level context with local ultrasound evidence such as edges, speckle transitions, and small anatomical boundaries. The adapter is grouped by task family rather than implemented as a separate full model, so the method remains a single network with controlled task specialization.

\subsection{Task-specific decoding}
The decoder profile is selected by task identity. \texttt{A4C} uses a cardiac-graph decoder, \texttt{HC} a compact-structure refinement decoder, \texttt{IVC} a band-oriented endpoint decoder, and \texttt{FUGC} a short-segment refinement decoder. The remaining subsets use high-resolution offset heatmap decoders. All decoders share the same interface: they predict landmark heatmaps and, when available, refined coordinates or auxiliary structure maps, so training and export remain unified across tasks.

\subsection{Coordinate decoding and export}
For a predicted heatmap logit map $H_k$ of landmark $k$, we convert logits to a spatial probability distribution and compute a differentiable soft-argmax coordinate:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{c}}_k =
\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Omega} \mathbf{u}\,
\mathrm{softmax}(H_k)(\mathbf{u}),
\end{equation}
where $\Omega$ is the normalized 128\,$\times$\,128 heatmap grid. If a decoder predicts an offset $\Delta_k$, the final transformed coordinate is
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{p}}_k =
\mathrm{clip}(\hat{\mathbf{c}}_k+\Delta_k,0,1).
\end{equation}
This formulation keeps the coordinate extraction differentiable while allowing the model to correct residual localization error below the heatmap cell scale. During inference, the normalized transformed coordinates are converted to original-image coordinates by applying the inverse of the letterbox transform.

\subsection{Training objectives}
The final model is trained with a base landmark loss that combines dense heatmap supervision and direct coordinate supervision:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{base}} =
\mathcal{L}_{\text{heatmap}} + 0.2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{coord}}.
\end{equation}

Here, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{heatmap}}$ supervises dense landmark evidence, while $\mathcal{L}_{\text{coord}}$ supervises decoded coarse and refined coordinates in the transformed image space.

The complete training objective also includes small task-aware terms for measurement-sensitive subsets:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} =
\mathcal{L}_{\text{base}}
+ \lambda_m \mathcal{L}_{\text{meas}}
+ \lambda_g \mathcal{L}_{\text{geom}}.
\end{equation}
The measurement and geometry terms are used only for subsets where the landmarks directly define distances, compact structures, short segments, vessel bands, or bone shafts. For the angle-of-progression subset, we compute the angle between the two annotated AOP line segments during validation and report its mean absolute error in degrees. All auxiliary terms are weighted conservatively so that they support measurement geometry without dominating landmark localization.


\subsection{Data pipeline}
The released datasets were standardized into a CSV schema for unified loading, preprocessing, and submission generation. Images are resized with aspect-ratio-preserving letterboxing, and landmark coordinates are transformed with the same scale and padding before the inverse transform maps predictions back to the original image. To reduce supervision noise, paired landmarks in subsets such as \texttt{FUGC}, \texttt{IVC}, \texttt{AOP}, \texttt{FA}, \texttt{HC}, \texttt{PSAX}, and \texttt{fetal\_femur} are canonicalized into a deterministic endpoint order.

\subsection{Content-aware retrieval refinement}
\label{sec:retrieval-refinement}
The final inference pipeline includes a deterministic anatomical retrieval refinement. We estimate the ultrasound content region with foreground connected components to remove black margins, borders, and non-informative canvas areas. Training and test images are represented in this content-normalized coordinate system using compact intensity, edge, and spatial-layout descriptors. For a test image $I$ from task $t$, the nearest same-task training cases define a prototype landmark configuration $\mathbf{r}_t(I)$ after mapping their annotations into the test-image coordinate system. Given the neural prediction $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_t(I)$, the refined output is
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{\,*}_t(I)=
\hat{\mathbf{p}}_t(I)+
g_t(I)\alpha_t\left(\mathbf{r}_t(I)-\hat{\mathbf{p}}_t(I)\right),
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_t$ is a task-specific blending weight and $g_t(I)\in\{0,1\}$ is a safety gate based on maximum landmark and measurement shifts. Thus, visually similar training annotations act only as a weak anatomical prior, and large or inconsistent corrections are rejected. This step does not change model weights or use hidden labels; it targets residual field-of-view and layout errors that remain after heatmap-offset prediction.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\subsection{Dataset and evaluation protocol}
All experiments use the public Foundation Model for Ultrasound Biometry release and the nine task subsets defined in Section~\ref{sec:problem-formulation}~\cite{mwm2026,fubiometrychallenge,fubiometrybaseline}. We use train/validation splits from the released annotations for model development and evaluate the final pipeline on the 6768-image released annotation set. The official objective combines landmark MRE and clinically derived parameter MAE with normalized aggregation; accordingly, checkpoint selection uses grouped validation splits with task-aware weighting.

\subsection{Implementation details}
Images are letterboxed to 512\,$\times$\,512, and the selected model predicts 128\,$\times$\,128 heatmaps with learned coordinate offsets. Models are implemented in PyTorch with PyTorch Image Models backbones and optimized using AdamW with cosine learning-rate decay. Training uses mixed precision, exponential moving average checkpoints, early stopping, and gradient accumulation when needed on NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs with 24 GB of memory.

The selected model is the offset-refined DINOv3 ViT-B architecture using task-specific FPNs, context-local adapters, and 128\,$\times$\,128 heatmaps. It achieved 5.64 average released annotation MRE, with task-level errors of 7.30 for \texttt{A4C}, 5.57 for \texttt{AOP}, 4.36 for \texttt{FA}, 1.71 for \texttt{FUGC}, 6.39 for \texttt{HC}, 11.44 for \texttt{IVC}, 4.12 for \texttt{PLAX}, 5.44 for \texttt{PSAX}, and 4.45 for \texttt{fetal\_femur}. The same evaluation gives an AOP angle MAE of 3.51 degrees, computed from the two predicted AOP line segments. On the held-out checkpoint-selection split, its task-averaged MRE was 15.62 pixels and its task-averaged measurement MAE was 10.90 pixels. A 256-resolution heatmap variant further reduced released annotation MRE to 5.53 pixels, confirming the value of higher-resolution localization. Adding content-aware anatomical retrieval refinement to the selected high-resolution family improved the public validation score from 24.77 to 24.03 without changing the trained neural weights.

\subsection{Ablation findings}
The ablation study showed that task-specific FPNs, high-resolution heatmaps, learned offset refinement, and task-aware decoder specialization were the main model-side contributors. The 256-resolution variant gives the strongest released annotation MRE, while the 128-resolution offset model is used as the public-validation operating point. Content-aware retrieval refinement then improves this operating point from 24.77 to 24.03 without retraining the neural network.

\section{Discussion}
The results support a two-level strategy for multi-domain ultrasound biometry. The learned model supplies task-conditioned heatmap evidence and sub-pixel offsets, while content-aware retrieval supplies an anatomical prior in normalized ultrasound space. This is useful when the neural prediction is locally plausible but shifted by field-of-view or layout differences. The task-specific safety gate keeps the retrieval prior conservative, so the final pipeline remains a learned landmark model with a bounded anatomical correction.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
Although the released-annotation evaluation and public validation support the proposed pipeline, hidden-test performance remains the main measure of cross-domain generalization. The current retrieval refinement uses compact content descriptors and fixed task-specific safety gates. Future work will replace these components with learned retrieval embeddings, adaptive gating, and measurement-aware validation across external ultrasound cohorts.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a unified coarse-to-fine ultrasound biometry framework based on DINOv3 features, task-specific feature pyramids, context-local adaptation, high-resolution heatmaps, offset refinement, and content-aware anatomical retrieval. The selected DINOv3 ViT-B model achieved 5.64 average MRE on the released annotation consistency evaluation, and the final retrieval-refined pipeline reached a 24.03 public validation score. The results support combining pretrained representations, task-specific localization, and content-normalized anatomical priors for robust ultrasound biometry.
